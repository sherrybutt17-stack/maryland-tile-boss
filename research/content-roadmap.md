## 0. Read this first

This roadmap is written for a **2–3 person business**, not a marketing department. Every item below has an honest effort estimate split into **owner time** (what Mike personally has to supply, because nobody else can) and **production time** (writing, photo prep, publishing).

Three rules govern everything in this document:

1. **Nothing gets published that can't be substantiated.** Where a piece depends on a claim that has not been verified — Schluter/Wedi certification, "since 2000", "25+ vendors", MHIC licence, radiant heat installation — it is marked **⚠️ CONFIRM FIRST** and must not be written until the owner confirms in writing. See §7.
2. **No review-dependent tactics.** The business has one verified public review and it is negative (`local-seo.md` §4). Nothing here assumes testimonials, star ratings, or `AggregateRating` schema.
3. **The differentiator is Mike, not the writing.** 45 years, third generation, and genuine waterproofing depth. Any piece that could have been written by someone who has never set a tile is not on this list — or is on the do-not-write list in §8.

**Realistic cadence: 1–2 published pieces per month.** That is 12–18 pieces in the first year. This roadmap contains 20 pieces plus a standing project series, which fits that cadence with no heroics. Anyone proposing four blog posts a week for this business is selling volume, not results.

### What content can and cannot fix

Content is **not** the highest-priority workstream at this business. `local-seo.md` establishes that the entity is fragmented across ~10 name variants, two phone numbers and two cities, and that five of seven domains are dead. Map-pack rankings — where the bottom-of-funnel money is for almost every keyword in `keywords.md` §2.1–2.3 — are gated on fixing that, not on publishing articles.

**However:** organic article rankings are *less* gated on NAP consistency than the map pack is. So content production can run in parallel with the entity cleanup, with one caveat — **the cost page (Piece 1) is a conversion page, and it will convert badly while the site shows two phone numbers and the hours conflict is unresolved.** Settle the phone and hours before that page goes live.

## 1. The scoring model

Every candidate is scored on three axes, 1–5 each. **Priority Score = Commercial Value × Ranking Opportunity × Business Relevance** (max 125). Multiplication, not addition, because a zero on any axis should kill a piece — a topic with no commercial value, no path to ranking, *or* no genuine connection to the business is not worth an afternoon.

### Commercial Value (CV) — how close is the reader to paying someone?

| Score | Meaning |
|:--:|---|
| 5 | Reader is choosing a supplier or installer right now |
| 4 | Reader is budgeting or shortlisting; likely to make contact within weeks |
| 3 | Reader has a live project but is still deciding scope or materials |
| 2 | Reader is browsing a project six-plus months out |
| 1 | No project — reading for interest, or explicitly doing it themselves |

### Ranking Opportunity (RO) — how winnable within 12 months?

Judged against the actual position: a **brand-new domain with essentially zero authority, one review, and a fragmented entity**. This is the axis where most content plans lie to themselves.

| Score | Meaning | Evidence source |
|:--:|---|---|
| 5 | SERP is doorway farms, call-centre subdomains and aggregators — no genuine local page exists | `competitors.md` §2.10, §2.7 |
| 4 | Weak local incumbents with thin pages, missing schema, no depth | `competitors.md` §4.2–4.9 |
| 3 | Real competitors with real pages; beatable only on depth and first-hand material | — |
| 2 | National publishers (This Old House, Home Depot, Bob Vila, Houzz) own the SERP | — |
| 1 | National publishers plus a Google answer box; no realistic path for a local site | — |

### Business Relevance (BR) — does this business actually do it, and does Mike have material nobody else has?

| Score | Meaning |
|:--:|---|
| 5 | Core service **and** Mike holds unique material — photos, jobsite war stories, real numbers |
| 4 | Core service; the material exists but has to be created from scratch |
| 3 | Adjacent to the business; honest to write but not distinctive |
| 2 | Tangential — writing it is a stretch |
| 1 | Not what they do, or requires an unverified claim to stand up |

### Where the score is deliberately overridden

The score sequences the list; it does not *dictate* the order. Two adjustments:

- **Pieces 14 and 15 score 36 but are in Phase 1.** They already exist as published posts (`business-data.md`) and need two hours each, not four days. Best return per hour on the whole list.
- **Piece 20 (porcelain vs. ceramic) scores 20 and is the sharpest demotion here.** It is the single most-written article in the tile industry; This Old House, Home Depot, Lowe's, Architectural Digest and every manufacturer own that SERP (RO 2). Written generically it is filler, and belongs in §8.
- **Piece 12 scores 50 on tiny query volume (RO 2) but sits in Phase 1** because it is the highest-value-per-lead audience in the business and the page already exists — this is a 2-hour upgrade to `/trade/`, not new content. It earns its slot on conversion, not traffic.

## 3. Phase 1 — first 90 days

**Target: 6 published items across 3 months.** Two are upgrades of existing posts, one is a page upgrade, so the true "from scratch" load is three pieces plus the first project write-up. That is deliverable at 2–3 staff.

### Month 1

**Piece 1 — What Tile Installation Actually Costs in Maryland (2026)**
- **URL:** `/tile-installation-cost-maryland/` — a top-level money page, **not** a blog post
- **Intent:** COMM, tipping to BOFU · **Primary keyword:** `tile installation cost Maryland`
- **Links up to:** every service page (`/tile-installation/bathroom/`, `/shower/`, `/backsplash/`, `/floors/`) links *to* it; it links back *down* to each, and ends on `/contact/`
- **Length:** 1,400–1,800 words, structured as ranges by job type with a short "what moves the price" section under each
- **Why first, despite being the biggest lift:** `competitors.md` §6 — *not one competitor in this market publishes pricing*. USA Pro Floors ranks for cost intent while giving no figures at all. `keywords.md` §1.6 confirms Google will rank a local contractor's own cost guide (PRG Contractors ranks with exactly this page type). Cost intent is the highest-converting non-branded intent in home services, and it is sitting unclaimed.
- **What makes it first-hand:** these are **Mike's own numbers, pulled off his own past invoices** — not scraped from a national calculator. Include what actually changes the price on a real Anne Arundel job: an out-of-level 1960s Annapolis floor, a shower that has to be torn back to studs, mud-set versus a foam system, herringbone versus straight-lay labour, waterfront crawlspace access. Say plainly what he does *not* do.
- **Owner time:** 90 minutes with a stack of past invoices and a notepad. Nobody else can produce this input.
- **🚨 Hard constraint:** publish the client's own ranges. The figures quoted in `keywords.md` §1.6 are other companies' numbers and must not be reused. Ranges, not single prices, and a visible "priced per job after we see it" line.
- **⚠️ Prerequisite:** resolve the phone-number and hours conflicts (`local-seo.md` §1c) before this page goes live. A cost page is a call-generating page; it should not point at two phone numbers.

**Pieces 14 + 15 — upgrade the two existing blog posts** (same month, they are small)
- `/blog/glass-tile-backsplash-pros-and-cons/` and `/blog/choosing-glass-tile-for-a-shower/`
- Both target real, live-autocomplete-confirmed queries (`keywords.md` §2.5). Both currently read as generic marketing copy (`keywords.md` §6.4).
- **The upgrade, in one line each:** add what Mike knows that a content writer doesn't — glass is unforgiving of a wavy substrate, it telegraphs the trowel notches through translucent tile, it needs a white setting material and the right trowel, and mesh-mounted sheets versus paper-face changes the whole job. Add one real photo from the Two Rivers or Bowie backsplash. Add an internal link up to `/tile-installation/backsplash/` and across to `/tile/glass-and-mosaic/`.
- **Effort:** 20 min owner / 2 h production each.

**Piece 12 — upgrade `/trade/` with the lock-box programme**
- Not new content; a rewrite of an existing page around the one genuinely verified differentiator nobody in this market has (`architecture-and-art-direction.md` §1, `competitors.md` §7.10).
- Spell out mechanically **how the lock-box actually works** — who qualifies, how access is granted, what hours, what a designer can do with a client in there unattended, how samples are checked out. Specificity is the whole value; a designer reading it needs to know whether it solves their Saturday-afternoon-client problem.
- **⚠️ CONFIRM FIRST:** do not claim a formal trade *pricing* programme unless one exists (`keywords.md` §6.1). "We work with designers, builders and contractors, and here is the access we give you" is fully substantiable on its own.

### Month 2

**Piece 2 — Seven Things I Find When I Tear Out a Failed Tile Shower**
- **URL:** `/blog/why-tile-showers-fail/` · **Intent:** INFO leading hard to BOFU
- **Primary keywords:** `why do tile showers leak` / `failed shower replacement Annapolis` / `leaking tile shower repair Annapolis` (`keywords.md` §2.8, §2.12)
- **Links up to:** `/tile-installation/shower/` (mandatory, high in the body), plus `/tile-installation-cost-maryland/`
- **Length:** 1,200–1,500 words. One numbered failure per section, each with a photo.
- **Why this is the single best piece on the roadmap:** it is the one article on this list that **literally cannot be written by anyone who has not done the work.** Every competitor and every content farm can write "shower waterproofing is important." Only someone who has demolished failed showers for 45 years can photograph the rotted blocking behind a pan liner that was never turned up the curb, the mesh tape at a plane change, the mortar bed poured over a folded liner, the missing pre-slope. `competitors.md` §2.9 shows an HVAC company outranking most tile firms for shower queries — the SERP is that weak.
- **What makes it first-hand:** the photos. Mike almost certainly has demo shots on his phone already; if not, the next teardown produces them for free. Real numbers help too — how many years the failure took to show, what the repair cost versus what doing it right would have cost.
- **Owner time:** 45 minutes as a voice memo on the drive back from a job, plus digging out photos.

**Piece 3 — What to Bring to a Tile Showroom (and What to Leave at Home)**

**Piece 6 — Shower Waterproofing Systems, Compared**
- **URL:** `/blog/shower-waterproofing-systems/` · **Intent:** INFO
- **Primary keywords:** `shower waterproofing systems`, `shower waterproofing membrane`, `sheet membrane vs liquid membrane shower` (`keywords.md` §2.8)
- **Links up to:** `/tile-installation/shower/` (mandatory, both directions)
- **Length:** 1,300–1,600 words

- **What makes it first-hand:** the trade-offs an installer knows and a manufacturer brochure won't print — where liquid-applied goes wrong (film thickness, corners, cure time before flood test), what a sheet membrane demands at the drain and the curb, which system suits a curbless build, which is faster on a retrofit over an existing floor, why the flood test is non-negotiable and how long he leaves it. A short "questions to ask whoever is quoting your shower" block is the citation-bait section.
- **⚠️ CONFIRM FIRST on brand naming.** Mike may name and discuss the systems he **installs** (`architecture-and-art-direction.md` §1 permits describing real practice). He may **not** be described as "Schluter-certified" or "Wedi Pro Certified" until that is confirmed — those are manufacturer-verified credentials and a false claim is reportable. Write around it: *"the systems I install on most of my showers"* is honest and loses nothing.

**Piece 5 — first project write-up** (then one per completed job, ongoing)
- **URL pattern:** `/portfolio/<project-slug>/` — the site already has five of these
- **Length:** 300–450 words plus 4–8 photos
- **Why this is the quiet workhorse:** `keywords.md` §6.2 establishes that project pages are the **honest** way to earn relevance for Bowie, Dunkirk, Odenton and every other town without building doorway pages. A real page about a real Severna Park bathroom with real photos cannot be penalised, and it is strictly better than a `/severna-park/` template page.
- **What makes it first-hand:** it is a photograph of work that happened. Include the town, the material and where it came from, one problem encountered and how it was solved, and roughly how long it took. That last bit — the problem — is what separates it from a gallery.
- **Effort:** 15 min owner / 1.5 h production. **This is the piece that keeps the cadence alive in a busy month.**

## 6. The pieces that could realistically earn links or AI citations

Most of this roadmap will never earn a link, and pretending otherwise wastes the owner's time. **Five pieces genuinely could.** They share one property: each contains information that does not exist anywhere else on the internet.

**1. `/tile-installation-cost-maryland/` — real published numbers**
Nobody in this market publishes pricing (`competitors.md` §6). Real, ranged, local cost data is the most linkable format in home services: remodelling roundups cite it, local journalists writing "what a bathroom reno costs in Anne Arundel" cite it, and AI answer engines cite it because it is a direct numeric answer to a directly numeric question. It is also the piece most likely to be *scraped* — which is fine; the citation usually comes with it.

**2. `/blog/why-tile-showers-fail/` — original teardown photography**
Original photographs of failure modes are genuinely scarce and genuinely shareable. This is the one piece with a realistic path to trade-community pickup (r/Tile, tile installer Facebook groups, John Bridge forums) and to manufacturer interest — waterproofing manufacturers actively look for real-world failure documentation, which also makes it the natural asset to send to a Schluter or Wedi rep when opening the dealer-locator conversation (see `backlink-plan.md` §2, §8).

**3. `/blog/shower-waterproofing-systems/` — the unclaimed technical topic**
`keywords.md` §2.8 found essentially no Anne Arundel contractor owning this. Structured comparison content with clear headings and direct answers is the canonical AI-citation format. Realistic outcome: cited in AI answers for waterproofing questions, and the page a local remodeler or plumber links to when they need to explain the difference to a client.

**4. `/blog/how-much-tile-to-order/` — a clean factual answer**
Short, numeric, unambiguous, and answerable in one paragraph. That is precisely what gets lifted into AI Overviews and assistant answers. It is also the natural seed for a simple order-quantity calculator later, which is a much stronger link asset — note that USA Pro Floors' cost calculator is described in `competitors.md` §4.1 as its most differentiated asset, and In Home Stone's 3D visualiser as the best interactive asset in the market. **Six of eight competitors have no interactive tool at all.**

**5. `/blog/tile-waterfront-homes-chesapeake/` — the local-press piece**
The only piece on this list with a plausible route to a genuine editorial placement. It is hyper-local, genuinely expert, and useful to a Bay-area readership — which makes it a real pitch to *Chesapeake Home + Living*, *What's Up? Annapolis*, *Bay Weekly* or *Eye On Annapolis* rather than a cold ask. See `backlink-plan.md` §6.

**Expectation setting:** across a year, a realistic outcome from these five is **two to five earned links plus meaningful AI-answer presence** — not a link-building programme. That is a good outcome for a 2–3 person business and is worth more than fifty directory listings.

## 9. How a 2–3 person business actually produces this

The plan fails if it depends on Mike sitting down to write. It won't happen. Build the production system around what he already does.

**The voice memo.** Every piece on this list needs 20–90 minutes of *Mike talking*, not writing. The drive back from a job is the slot. One memo per piece, recorded on his phone, sent to whoever is producing. Transcription is free and near-perfect now. The writer's job is to shape the transcript, not to invent content — which is exactly why these pieces read as first-hand and the competition's don't.

**The photo habit.** Thirty seconds per job: one wide shot before, two or three during (substrate, waterproofing, layout), three or four after. This single habit feeds the project series (Piece 5), the failure piece (Piece 2), the waterproofing piece (Piece 6), and GBP's monthly photo requirement (`local-seo.md` §3.5) — four workstreams off one habit. Photograph the failures too; those are the rarest and most valuable images in this business.

**Batch the owner time.** One 90-minute session per month covering two pieces beats six scattered 15-minute interruptions.

**The escape valve.** In a month where the work is heavy, publish a project write-up instead of the scheduled piece. 15 minutes of owner time, still genuinely useful, still earns local relevance. **Never publish filler to hit a cadence** — an empty month costs nothing; a generic post costs credibility and adds a page that dilutes the site.

**Repurposing, free.** Every piece becomes a GBP post (2 sentences plus the link), a Facebook post, and one or two seeded GBP Q&A entries (`local-seo.md` §3.6). Same content, three more surfaces, ten minutes.

## 10. Measurement and the kill switch

Judge this roadmap on four things only:

1. **Showroom visits and quote requests attributed to content pages** — the only metric that matters. Ask every caller how they found you and write it down. At this volume, a paper log beats an analytics dashboard.
2. **Impressions in Google Search Console per published piece** — check at 90 days, not before. Content takes a quarter to register on a new domain.
3. **AI-answer presence** — every quarter, ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Mode: *"how much does tile installation cost in Maryland"*, *"why do tile showers leak"*, *"tile showroom near Annapolis"*. Record whether the business is named. This is a real and currently uncontested channel in this market.
4. **Earned links** — from the five pieces in §6 only. Expect 2–5 in year one.

**The kill switch:** if a *category* of piece produces no impressions after six months, stop writing that category. If the cost page and the waterproofing pieces perform while the ideas/trends pieces don't — which is the likely outcome — reallocate everything to cost, waterproofing, technical installation depth, and project write-ups. **Follow the evidence over this document.**
